## A. Forecasting benchmarks and evaluation

### A1 — Naive, seasonal-naive and drift are the standard benchmark set
- **Status:** `PROSPECTIVE`
- **Claimed source:** Hyndman & Athanasopoulos, *Forecasting: Principles and
  Practice*, 3rd ed., §5.2
- **Used in:** `srq1-model-ladder-and-baselines.md`; justifies why four benchmarks
  were added 2026-08-22
- **Confidence:** High. FPP3 is open-access at otexts.com/fpp3, so this is cheap to
  verify directly.
- **To check:** that §5.2 is the right section number in the 3rd edition, and the
  exact wording about these being standard benchmarks.

### A2 — Sophisticated methods frequently fail to beat simple benchmarks
- **Status:** `PROSPECTIVE`
- **Claimed source:** Makridakis, Spiliotis & Assimakopoulos, M4 competition results
  (*International Journal of Forecasting*, 2018 and 2020)
- **Used in:** the RTD finding — seasonal-naive (27.3% WMAPE) beats every tuned model
  (35.1-37.0%)
- **Confidence:** High on the substance; **the 2018 vs 2020 split needs checking**
  (M4 results vs M5, and which paper carries which claim).
- **Why it matters:** this is the citation that turns an embarrassing result into a
  contextualised one. Worth getting exactly right.

### A3 — Rolling-origin / expanding-window evaluation for time series
- **Status:** `PROSPECTIVE`
- **Claimed sources:** (a) Hyndman & Athanasopoulos §5.10 "Time series
  cross-validation"; (b) Tashman (2000), *IJF* 16(4), on rolling-origin evaluation
- **Used in:** `srq1-tuning-and-validation-protocol.md`; justifies expanding-window CV
- **Confidence:** High on (a). **(b) needs verification** — volume, issue, year and
  whether it is the right paper for this specific claim.

### A4 — K-fold CV is invalid for time series
- **Status:** `UNSOURCED`
- **Used in:** the methodology argument that shuffling permits training on later
  months than the one predicted
- **Note:** this may be implicit in A3 rather than a separate citable claim. Worth
  asking NotebookLM whether a source states the invalidity explicitly, or whether it
  should be presented as a logical consequence of the forecasting setup instead.

### A5 — WMAPE / weighted MAPE as an operational forecasting metric
- **Status:** `UNSOURCED`
- **Used in:** throughout; WMAPE is the headline metric
- **Needed:** a source defining WMAPE and motivating volume weighting for demand
  forecasting. Also relevant: whether the literature has a standard name (WMAPE vs
  weighted MAPE vs WAPE).

### A6 — MAPE is undefined against zero actuals; median APE is the robust alternative
- **Status:** `UNSOURCED`
- **Used in:** the scorability filter (40% of brands excluded), the medMAPE headline
  for statistical baselines, and the `test_mape` = 9.3e10 defect
- **Needed:** a source on MAPE's failure modes. Hyndman & Koehler (2006), "Another
  look at measures of forecast accuracy", *IJF* 22(4), is the obvious candidate —
  **but treat as `PROSPECTIVE` only once someone confirms it.**

## B. Hyperparameter optimisation

### B1 — TPE as a Bayesian hyperparameter sampler
- **Status:** `PROSPECTIVE`
- **Claimed source:** Bergstra, Bardenet, Bengio & Kegl (2011), "Algorithms for
  Hyper-Parameter Optimization", NeurIPS
- **Confidence:** High.

### B2 — Random search outperforms grid search
- **Status:** `PROSPECTIVE`
- **Claimed source:** Bergstra & Bengio (2012), "Random Search for Hyper-Parameter
  Optimization", *JMLR* 13
- **Confidence:** High.
- **Note:** only needed if the thesis explains *why not grid search*.

### B3 — Optuna's design (define-by-run, pruning)
- **Status:** `PROSPECTIVE`
- **Claimed source:** Akiba, Sano, Yanase, Ohta & Koyama (2019), "Optuna: A
  Next-generation Hyperparameter Optimization Framework", KDD
- **Confidence:** High.

### B4 — "Convention for HPO trial count is 50-200"
- **Status:** `UNSOURCEABLE`
- **Origin:** asserted by an assistant 2026-08-22 as though it were established.
  **It is not.** No paper prescribes a trial count; the requirement depends on the
  search space.
- **Resolution already implemented:** `srq1_benchmark_cv.py` saves the convergence
  curve and reports the trial at which the objective plateaued. The write-up argues
  *"the objective plateaued after N trials, indicating the budget was sufficient"* —
  an empirical claim shown in a figure, which a grader cannot challenge as a misread
  source.
- **Still worth asking NotebookLM:** whether any source discusses *how to determine*
  an adequate HPO budget (convergence-based stopping rules). That would let the
  empirical argument cite a method rather than stand alone.

### B5 — Nested CV is required for unbiased performance estimation
- **Status:** `UNSOURCED`
- **Relevance:** the protocol selects hyperparameters by CV and evaluates on a
  held-out test split — standard, but not fully nested, so the reported test score is
  mildly optimistic. This is stated as a limitation.
- **Needed:** a source for the optimism claim. Cawley & Talbot (2010), *JMLR*, on
  overfitting in model selection is the candidate — **verify before citing.**

## C. Modelling choices

### C1 — Regularised linear models as a standard tabular baseline
- **Status:** `PROSPECTIVE`
- **Claimed source:** Hastie, Tibshirani & Friedman, *Elements of Statistical
  Learning*, ch. 3
- **Used in:** justifying the Ridge rung
- **Confidence:** High that ESL ch. 3 covers ridge regression; **lower** that it
  frames it as "a standard baseline for comparison", which is the actual claim.

### C2 — Feature importance (SHAP) is not feature selection
- **Status:** `UNSOURCED`
- **Used in:** `prometheus-scenarios-design-rationale.md` — `weighted_dist` ranked
  #2 by SHAP yet dropping it improved accuracy in 3 of 4 categories
- **Needed:** a source distinguishing attribution from selection. Lundberg & Lee
  (2017) for SHAP itself is straightforward; the *"high importance does not imply
  keep it"* point needs its own source.
- **Note:** the thesis has its own measured instance, so this could stand as an
  empirical finding with SHAP cited only for the method.

### C3 — Split-conformal prediction intervals
- **Status:** `UNSOURCED`
- **Used in:** `forecast_tool.py` — every forecast carries a 90% conformal interval
- **Needed:** the conformal prediction reference. Lei et al. (2018) "Distribution-Free
  Predictive Inference for Regression" (*JASA*) or Shafer & Vovk (2008) are
  candidates — **unverified.**
- **Priority: HIGH.** This is a core SRQ2 claim (uncertainty quantification) and
  currently has no source at all.

### C4 — Pooled vs per-category modelling / transfer across related series
- **Status:** `UNSOURCED`
- **Used in:** `srq1-pooled-vs-per-category.md` — the crossover at ~750-1000 rows
- **Needed:** literature on global vs local forecasting models. The M5 competition
  and work on global neural forecasting models (e.g. Montero-Manso & Hyndman on
  "principles and algorithms for forecasting groups of time series") are candidates.
- **Value:** would place the finding in a literature rather than presenting it as
  novel. **Likely the highest-value gap in this register** — the result is good and
  currently uncontextualised.

### C5 — Log transformation for multiplicative demand relationships
- **Status:** `UNSOURCED`
- **Used in:** F54 — raw-unit features against a logged target broke Ridge; trees are
  immune because they split on rank order
- **Needed:** a source for the additive-vs-multiplicative point and for
  transformation invariance in tree-based models.
