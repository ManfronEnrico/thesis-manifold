## Detailed Methodological Analysis

### 1. The Geometry of Absolute Loss and the Median (FM-01)
Symmetric absolute-error loss ($S(x, y) = \vert x - y \vert$) is minimized pointwise by the **median** of the predictive distribution, as demonstrated by Gneiting (2011, p. 746). This forms the theoretical foundation for using Mean Absolute Error (MAE) in point forecast evaluations. If the goal of an organization is to minimize MAE, the optimal strategy for the forecaster is to report the median of their predictive distribution, rather than the mean or the mode. 

### 2. The Danger of Percentage Loss and the $(-1)$-Median (FM-03)
Pointwise optimization of percentage-error loss ($S(x, y) = \left\vert \frac{x-y}{y} \right\vert$) does **not** lead to the standard median. Rather, Gneiting (2011, p. 746) proves that it is consistent for the **$\beta$-median** where $\beta = -1$.
The $(-1)$-median functional, denoted as $med^{(-1)}(F)$, is the median of a probability distribution whose density is re-weighted proportionally to $y^{-1}$. Because dividing by smaller values amplifies the penalty, the optimal point forecast under MAPE is heavily shrunken toward zero.
*   **The practical consequence:** If models are trained or hyperparameter-tuned to directly minimize MAPE, they will systematically and severely **underforecast** compared to the true median or mean of the demand. 

### 3. Resolving the WMAPE/WMAPE and MAE Relationship (FM-02 & FM-03)
While APE pointwise minimizes the $(-1)$-median, WMAPE (Weighted Mean Absolute Percentage Error) is a **portfolio-level aggregate metric**:
$$\text{WMAPE} = \frac{\sum_{t=1}^T \vert Y_t - F_t \vert}{\sum_{t=1}^T \vert Y_t \vert}$$
When evaluated over a fixed evaluation set, the denominator $\sum_{t=1}^T \vert Y_t \vert$ is a constant determined entirely by the historical demand. Therefore, minimizing WMAPE over a fixed sample is mathematically equivalent to minimizing the sum of absolute errors (MAE). WMAPE optimization is therefore consistent with the standard median ($med^{(0)}$), making it safe from the severe downward bias of standard pointwise MAPE.

### 4. Zero actuals and percentage error degeneracy (FM-04 & FM-06)
Hyndman and Koehler (2006, p. 683) highlight that MAPE has a division-by-zero degeneracy when actual demand $Y_t = 0$. Under intermittent demand, such zero observations are extremely common. 
*   **Folklore Refuted:** Some research groups or software systems justify **excluding** forecast windows or time series containing zero actuals to make MAPE computable. Hyndman and Koehler (2006, p. 683) directly contradict this, labeling it an "artificial solution that is impossible to apply in practical situations."
*   **The Recommended Path:** Instead of altering the dataset to accommodate the metric, researchers must use metrics that are naturally defined at zero. Hyndman and Koehler propose **MASE**, which scales the error by the in-sample mean absolute error of a naïve model. For multi-series supply chain environments where volume-weighting is required, **WMAPE** is also mathematically robust at zero because the aggregation occurs *before* division.

## Safe, Thesis-Ready Formulations

*   **For the Metric Selection Justification:**
    > "Percentage-error measures such as MAPE are mathematically degenerate when actual demand is zero, and they exhibit extreme instability and skewness when actual values are close to zero (Hyndman & Koehler, 2006, p. 683). While some forecasting systems or competitions artificially exclude zero-sales windows to make MAPE computable, this is an artificial solution that is impractical for real-world inventory networks (Hyndman & Koehler, 2006, p. 681, 683)."

*   **For the WMAPE and Median Alignment Defense:**
    > "Under absolute-error loss, the optimal point forecast is strictly the median of the predictive distribution (Gneiting, 2011, p. 746). Pointwise optimization of Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE) is consistent for the nonstandard $(-1)$-median functional, which introduces a severe downward bias and leads to systematic underforecasting (Gneiting, 2011, p. 746, 752). However, because the Weighted Mean Absolute Percentage Error (WMAPE) aggregates absolute errors across the portfolio before dividing by the total volume, minimizing WMAPE over a fixed set of actuals is mathematically equivalent to minimizing Mean Absolute Error (MAE), which remains strictly consistent for the standard median of the predictive distribution (Gneiting, 2011, p. 752, 758)."

*   **For the MASE Baseline Baseline:**
    > "To compare forecast accuracy across multiple time series with significantly different scales, we utilize the Mean Absolute Scaled Error (MASE) proposed by Hyndman and Koehler (2006, p. 684-685). MASE scales the out-of-sample forecast error of each series by the in-sample mean absolute error of a one-step naïve (random walk) forecast, providing a robust, scale-free, and zero-stable metric."
