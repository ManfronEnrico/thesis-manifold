# Storyline

## The spine

> **A dedicated forecasting layer must be justified on reproducibility, cost and
> auditability, not on error alone.**

Every beat below is set up to make this sentence feel earned when it's said on slide 31.
If a beat doesn't push toward it, it's a candidate to cut.

## How the beats hand over

- **2 → 3** "That's the practical gap. Enrico will show it's an academic one too."
- **4 → 5** "Four questions about *designing* something make a design problem, not a
  hypothesis test, which is why the method is design science."
- **5 → 6** "So what did we build? Brian."
- **7 → 8** "That's what we measured. Enrico, what does it mean?"
- **8 → 9** "And here's what bounds all of that. Brian."

---

## The one tension to manage

Beat 7 tells the examiners that **our step loses on median accuracy** (14.6 vs 2.9), and
Scenario D's 0.9% is on screen. That's deliberate: it's the honest result, and the thesis
frames it the same way (Ch9 §9.2.3, *"wins on the axis usually reported and loses on three
that usually are not"*). Say it before they do. A surprise you name yourself reads as a
finding; one an examiner points out reads as a weakness.

### R1, the "systematic review" contradiction

The thesis was submitted on 15 Sep 2026, so it can't be changed now. Prepare the answer and
don't raise it first. Slide 8 already uses Chapter 2's wording, which is the correct one. If
asked, Enrico says:

> "Chapter 2's description is the accurate one: a structured, iterative search, not a
> systematic-review protocol. 'Systematic' in Chapter 3 is loose wording on our part."

Acknowledge it once and move on. Don't argue that "systematic" could also be read loosely.
