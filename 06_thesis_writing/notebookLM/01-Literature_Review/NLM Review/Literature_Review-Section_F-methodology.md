## Detailed Claim-by-Claim Breakdown

### Claim ID: LR-36 — Hevner's Design Science Framework

*   **Draft Statement:**
    > *"distinguishing the construction of a working artefact from the behavioural study of existing systems, and articulating guidelines that require an artefact to be both demonstrably useful in a relevant problem context and a source of generalisable knowledge beyond its specific instantiation."*
*   **Verdict:** **Supported**
*   **Exact Source Location:** Hevner et al., 2004, *MIS Quarterly*, 28(1), pp. 75–105.
*   **Verbatim Supporting Quote:**
    > *"The goal of behavioral-science research is truth. The goal of design-science research is utility... Truth informs design and utility informs theory."* (p. 78)
    > *"Guideline 1: Design as an Artifact... Guideline 2: Problem Relevance... Guideline 7: Communication of Research..."* (p. 83)
*   **Assumptions & Mathematical Constraints:**
    *   The framework establishes 7 guidelines for conducting and evaluating high-quality design-science research.

#### Critical Scrutiny & Thesis Risk Analysis
*   The thesis matches Hevner's philosophy perfectly: constructing a forecasting-decision artifact (utility-oriented) that is evaluated in an operational context, while deriving design principles that are generalizable to other FMCG/SME supply chains.

#### Safest Thesis-Ready Wording
```latex
Following the Design Science Research (DSR) paradigm formulated by Hevner et al. (2004), this thesis distinguishes the pragmatic construction and evaluation of a decision-support artifact (the utility-driven DSR objective) from the behavioral study of existing systems. Our artifact design adheres to Hevner's guidelines, demonstrating operational utility within a relevant SCM context while contributing generalizable, design-oriented knowledge to the SCM literature.
```

### Claim ID: LR-37 — Peffers's DSRM Process

*   **Draft Statement:**
    > *"the six-step process — problem identification, objective definition, design and development, demonstration, evaluation, and communication."*
*   **Verdict:** **Supported**
*   **Exact Source Location:** Peffers et al., 2007, *Journal of Management Information Systems*, 24(3), pp. 45–77.
*   **Verbatim Supporting Quote:**
    > *"The DSRM process includes six steps: (1) problem identification and motivation, (2) definition of the objectives for a solution, (3) design and development, (4) demonstration, (5) evaluation, and (6) communication."* (p. 45, 54-56)
*   **Assumptions & Mathematical Constraints:**
    *   Provides a standardized methodology for researchers to present, evaluate, and structure design science outcomes in information systems.

#### Critical Scrutiny & Thesis Risk Analysis
*   The steps, order, and names are verbatim correct. Peffers et al. (2007) is the correct citation and serves as the structural outline of the entire thesis.

#### Safest Thesis-Ready Wording
```latex
This study adopts the six-stage Design Science Research Methodology (DSRM) process model developed by Peffers et al. (2007) to structure our research lifecycle, spanning: (1) problem identification and motivation, (2) definition of the objectives for a solution, (3) design and development, (4) demonstration, (5) evaluation, and (6) communication.
```
